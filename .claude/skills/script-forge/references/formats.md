# Format playbook

One section per format `radar.py` labels. Each gives the beat structure with
timing for a ~30 s video (scale for other lengths), what makes it work, and
the usual way it fails. Use the reference's `STYLE_DNA.md` to adjust pace and
re-hook spacing.

## pov
- 0-1.5 s  on-screen "POV: <a situation the viewer has lived>", no talking or one line
- 1.5-20 s acted moment, 3-6 cuts, the relatable detail is the joke
- end      the twist or the too-real line; loops well
- Works because: recognition ("this is literally me") drives shares and tags.
- Fails when: the situation is too general. "POV: you're tired" < "POV: you said 'one more game' at 11pm and it's 3am".

## storytime
- 0-2 s    start at the most dramatic moment, past tense: "I got fired over a spreadsheet."
- 2-8 s    one line of context, then the stakes
- 8-24 s   escalation in 2-3 beats, each opening on "so", "but", "and then"
- 24-30 s  the payoff, then stop; or loop to line one
- Works because: an open question ("how?") the viewer only closes by staying.
- Fails when: it starts at the beginning chronologically.

## tutorial
- 0-2 s    show the end result first, then "here's how" in the same breath
- 2-25 s   3-5 steps, each one line + one visual; number them on screen
- 25-30 s  the gotcha most people miss (the save trigger)
- Works because: saves. The result-first open earns the steps.
- Fails when: step one is setup nobody cares about.

## list
- 0-2 s    the count and the stakes: "3 settings that are costing you views"
- order    second-best first, weakest in the middle, best last (or best first if the hook promises it)
- each     item name on screen, one sentence why, one example
- Works because: the count is a progress bar; viewers stay to finish it.
- Fails when: items are generic. Each needs a number or a named example.

## mistakes-myths
- 0-2 s    name the mistake with "you": "You're posting at the wrong time."
- 2-10 s   why it's wrong, with one proof point
- 10-25 s  the fix, concrete
- end      the consequence of not fixing it, or "send this to someone who does this"
- Works because: mild threat + easy fix. Shares to friends.
- Fails when: it scolds. Keep it on your side of the table.

## versus
- 0-2 s    the matchup and a stake: "$20 vs $200 headset for horror games"
- middle   alternate rounds, 2-4 criteria, a score on screen
- end      the verdict and the one case where the loser wins
- Works because: people pick a side in comments.

## challenge
- 0-2 s    the challenge and the scale: "I played only horror games for 7 days."
- middle   day/hour markers on screen, one moment per marker, the low point in the middle
- end      the result and one surprising takeaway
- Works because: time-lapse progress plus a stake.

## day-in-life
- 0-2 s    the twist that makes this day worth watching (job, place, number)
- middle   time stamps on screen, 6-10 clips, one specific detail each
- end      the honest line (cost, tiredness, the real reason)
- Fails when: it's a routine with no reason to watch.

## before-after
- 0-1 s    the after (or the before with "wait for it" energy, no words needed)
- middle   the process compressed, 3-5 steps
- end      side by side
- Works because: payoff is visual and instant.

## reaction
- 0-2 s    your face + the clip's most extreme moment
- middle   pause the clip, add the thing only you know
- end      verdict
- Rule: add real commentary; the original creator's clip is short and credited.

## explainer
- 0-2 s    the question the viewer already has, or the surprising fact
- middle   the explanation as a story or an analogy, not a definition
- end      the "so that's why" line
- Fails when: it opens with a definition.

## review
- 0-2 s    the verdict first: "I paid $60 so you don't have to."
- middle   two things it does well, one deal-breaker, all specific
- end      who should buy it and who shouldn't

## hot-take
- 0-2 s    the opinion, stated flatly, no hedging: "Jump scares ruined horror games."
- middle   two reasons with examples, one concession
- end      a question that splits the audience (honest, not bait)
- Works because: comments. Be right enough to defend it.

## skit
- characters labelled on screen, one costume/prop cue each
- 0-2 s    the setup line from the character the viewer is
- beats    escalation, every 3-5 s
- end      the punchline, then a 1 s reaction shot (rewatches)

## satisfying
- no talking, or one line; the sound design is the script
- the most satisfying moment in the first 2 s, then repeat with variation

## behind-scenes
- 0-2 s    the finished thing, then "this took 40 hours"
- middle   the ugly middle; the mistake you fixed
- end      back to the finished thing

## Retention mechanics (all formats)

- **Open loop** in the first 3 seconds: a question, a count, a promised result.
- **Re-hook** every 8-10 s (or the reference's spacing): a turn word, a
  pattern interrupt (cut, zoom, new location, on-screen number).
- **Payoff before the end**, then end within 1-2 s: the slow fade kills
  replays.
- **Loop**: last line flows into the first line ("...and that's why I never
  touch this setting." -> "I lost $300 on this setting.").
- **One ask**, matched to the format: tutorial -> save; hot-take -> comment;
  mistakes -> send to a friend; story -> follow for part 2.
